Frequently asked questions

Answers to the most common questions about Pyrae — and a place to send your suggestions, bug reports, and feedback.

Questions

What is Pyrae?

Pyrae is an early wildfire detection platform. It combines photo reports from citizens, AI fire/smoke analysis, live NASA satellite data, and predictive spread modelling into a single live map of active fires and their risk.

How do I report a fire?

Tap the Report button on the map, attach a photo of what you see, and let the app use your location (or pick the spot manually). Your report is screened automatically and, if confirmed, appears on the map.

How accurate is the detection?

Every photo is screened by a computer-vision model that outputs confidence scores for fire and smoke. Reports are additionally cross-checked against satellite hotspot data. High-confidence detections are shown as fires; lower-confidence ones are flagged for review. Like any detection system, it isn't perfect — always treat the map as a decision aid, not the final word.

Are my photos and location public?

Confirmed reports — including the photo and location — are shown publicly on the map to help others. You can see the full details in our privacy policy.

Why do some fires disappear from the map?

Fires disappear when they stop being detected — by satellite, by new reports, or both — for an extended period. That usually means the fire has been contained or is no longer active. Fires also age out after 48 hours on the public map.

What is the "Fire Grid" heatmap?

The Fire Grid is a Bayesian probability field showing where each fire is likely to spread. The hotter the color, the higher the model's confidence that area will burn, based on wind, terrain, fuel moisture, and observed detections.

The stats panel shows two area metrics derived from this grid: Burned area counts cells with a burn probability above 25% — the high-confidence active fire zone — while Risk area counts cells above 5%, capturing the broader zone where fire may spread. Together they give you the fire's core and its potential reach.

Who is Pyrae for?

Everyone: citizens who want to report what they see, communities near fire-prone areas, and professionals — utilities, forestry operators, and emergency agencies — who need an earlier common operating picture.

Is Pyrae an emergency service?

No. Pyrae is an early-warning and situational-awareness tool. If you are in danger, always call your local emergency number first.
