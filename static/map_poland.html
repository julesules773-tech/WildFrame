Wildfires in Poland — live situation

Every year, thousands of wildfires break out across Poland — from spring grass and stubble fires to summer forest fires in regions such as Bory Dolnośląskie, Puszcza Notecka, and the Augustów and Białowieża forests. Early detection is the difference between a small burn and a large one: the sooner a fire is reported, the sooner responders can act.

Pyrae watches Poland in near real time using three independent signals. First, NASA FIRMS satellite data (VIIRS 375 m) detects thermal anomalies from space on every overpass. Second, citizen photo reports — anyone on the ground can upload a photo of a fire or smoke plume, which is screened automatically by an AI model for visible fire and smoke before it reaches the map. Third, a Bayesian Fire Grid fuses all evidence into a probability surface that shows where each fire is burning and how it is likely to spread given wind and fuel conditions.

How to read the map

Each tracked fire appears as a dot whose size reflects its burn probability. When you zoom in, the dot becomes a full heatmap blob: the warmer the colour, the higher the model's confidence that the area is burning. The Fire Grid also estimates the likely spread direction from live wind data, so you can see which towns, roads and forests are at risk as a fire grows.

Reports are shown with their source — citizen report, satellite hotspot, or confirmed government alert — and each fire's probability is displayed alongside live wind speed and direction. Road risk assessment marks roads near an active fire by how soon the fire could reach them.

Report a fire you can see

If you see smoke or flames, open the report panel on the map, attach a photo and your location. The AI screen runs instantly and the report is added to the Fire Grid, corroborated against satellite data where available. You can also read our FAQ, learn who we are, or contact us for partnerships with fire services, forestry and government agencies.

Pyrae is an information tool, not an emergency service. If a fire threatens life or property, call your local emergency number (112 in Poland).
