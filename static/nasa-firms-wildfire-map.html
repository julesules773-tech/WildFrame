What is NASA FIRMS?

NASA's Fire Information for Resource Management System (FIRMS) provides near-real-time active fire data from the MODIS and VIIRS satellite instruments. The VIIRS 375 m sensor detects thermal anomalies — areas significantly warmer than their surroundings — that often indicate active wildfires, agricultural burning, or industrial flaring.

FIRMS data is refreshed every 3 hours for most regions, making it one of the most reliable global sources for fire detection. However, satellite detection has inherent limitations: cloud cover, small fires and nighttime conditions can mask thermal signals, and the revisit interval means a fast-growing fire may already be large by the time it appears on the next pass.

How Pyrae uses FIRMS data

🛰️

Satellite corroboration

Every citizen photo report is checked against live NASA FIRMS hotspots. If a photo sighting falls within 3 km of a satellite detection in the past 12 hours, the report is confirmed — turning a single ground observation into a verified incident.

🧠

AI screening first

Before reaching the satellite corroboration step, every photo is screened by a computer-vision model for visible fire and smoke. Only AI-positive reports are checked against FIRMS — reducing noise and ensuring that confirmed fires have both ground truth and satellite evidence.

📈

Bayesian modelling

FIRMS hotspots feed directly into the Bayesian Fire Grid, which models how each fire is likely to spread. Satellite data provides the initial evidence; the grid fuses it with weather, terrain and vegetation data to produce a probabilistic spread forecast.

Why speed matters

Wildfires can double in size every 30–60 minutes under dry, windy conditions. Traditional satellite-only detection — while valuable — leaves a gap: the fire must be large enough and hot enough to trigger a thermal anomaly on the next satellite pass. By combining citizen ground reports with satellite corroboration, Pyrae fills this gap — detecting fires that are still small enough for initial attack.

The corroboration step (satellite + ground truth) is critical: it prevents false alarms from reaching responders while ensuring that real fires — even those the satellite missed on its last pass — are confirmed through independent evidence.

Data sources

  • NASA FIRMS — VIIRS 375 m and MODIS 1 km active fire detections, refreshed every 3 hours.
  • EFFIS / Copernicus — European Forest Fire Information System for fire weather indices.
  • Open-Meteo — real-time wind, temperature and humidity for spread modelling.
  • Citizen photo reports — ground-truth sightings screened by AI and validated against FIRMS.
