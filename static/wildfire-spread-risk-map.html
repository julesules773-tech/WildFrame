What is the Bayesian Fire Grid?

The Bayesian Fire Grid is a probabilistic model that estimates the likelihood of burning for every location near an active fire. Unlike a simple perimeter drawn around a fire's current extent, the grid models how the fire is likely to evolve — producing a probability surface rather than a hard boundary.

Each cell in the grid has a burn probability between 0 and 1. The model updates continuously as new evidence arrives: citizen reports, satellite hotspots, and live weather data. Cells with higher probability are shown in warmer colours on the map — red for high risk, yellow for lower risk.

How the spread model works

🌬️

Wind direction & speed

Live wind data from Open-Meteo determines the primary spread direction. Fires spread faster with the wind and slower against it — the model weights cell probabilities accordingly.

⛰️

Terrain & elevation

Fires spread faster uphill as pre-heated fuel above the fire front ignites more quickly. Terrain slope and aspect are factored into each cell's spread rate.

🌲

Vegetation & fuel type

Different vegetation types burn at different rates. Grass fires spread fast but burn out quickly; dense forest fires burn hotter but move more slowly.

💧

Humidity & fire weather

Low humidity dries out fuel and increases fire intensity. Live humidity, temperature and fire weather index data adjust the model's spread rate.

Burned area vs. risk area

The Fire Grid distinguishes between two key concepts:

  • Burned area — cells with very high probability (typically > 0.85) where the fire is likely already burning or has recently burned. These represent the fire's current footprint.
  • Risk area — cells with moderate to high probability (0.1 – 0.85) where the fire could spread in the coming hours. This is the forward-looking zone: the area that responders should monitor, evacuate or prepare.

The risk area is always larger than the burned area — and that's the point. By the time a fire reaches a location, it's too late to act. The risk area gives responders a head start: which roads to close, which communities to alert, where to position resources.

Road risk assessment

The Fire Grid doesn't just model vegetation — it also assesses which roads are at risk. By overlaying the spread probability surface onto the road network, Pyrae identifies which evacuation routes and access roads could be threatened by fire spread.

This means incident commanders can see, in real time, which roads are safe to use and which ones may be cut off — before the fire reaches them. Pre-positioning resources on the safe side of the risk zone is far more effective than reacting after roads are blocked.

Why predictive modelling matters

Knowing where a fire is matters. Knowing where it's going saves lives. Traditional fire maps show a static snapshot — the current perimeter, the latest satellite hotspot. But a fire is a moving, evolving event. The spread model extends detection into forecasting, giving communities and responders the most valuable thing: time.

Every hour of advance warning is an hour to evacuate residents, close roads, position firefighting resources, and shut down critical infrastructure. The Bayesian Fire Grid turns raw detection data into actionable intelligence — not just alerts.
