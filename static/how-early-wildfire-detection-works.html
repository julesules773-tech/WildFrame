HOW IT WORKS

How early wildfire detection works

Catching wildfires early is the single highest-leverage investment in fire resilience. Here's how modern detection works — from the first sighting on the ground to a confirmed, predicted incident on the map.

The detection pipeline

1️⃣

Citizen sighting

A person on the ground sees smoke or flames and opens the Pyrae map on their phone. They attach a photo and share their location — a process that takes seconds. This ground-truth sighting is the fastest possible detection signal: it requires no satellite pass, no infrastructure, just someone who is there.

2️⃣

AI screening

The photo is immediately screened by a computer-vision model (YOLOv26 + EfficientNet-B0 ensemble) that detects visible fire and smoke. The model returns per-class confidence scores for flame and smoke — not a binary yes/no. This runs locally on the server in under a second.

3️⃣

Satellite corroboration

The report is checked against live NASA FIRMS satellite hotspot data. If a photo sighting falls within 3 km of a satellite detection in the past 12 hours, the report is confirmed automatically — independent ground + space evidence. Reports without satellite match remain pending for human review.

4️⃣

Bayesian modelling

Confirmed fires feed into a Bayesian Fire Grid that models how each fire is likely to spread — factoring in wind speed and direction, terrain, vegetation type and humidity. The grid produces a probability surface showing which areas are most at risk in the coming hours.

Why each source matters

  • Citizen reports catch fires that satellites miss — small ignitions, fires under cloud cover, or fires in the gap between satellite passes. They are the fastest signal but need validation.
  • AI screening filters noise automatically. Without it, every submitted photo — fire or not — would require manual review. The model's 100% recall ensures no real fire is discarded.
  • Satellite corroboration turns a single ground observation into a confirmed incident. Two independent sources (ground + space) are far more reliable than either alone.
  • Predictive modelling extends detection into forecasting. Knowing where a fire is matters; knowing where it's going saves lives and resources.

Why speed saves lives

Wildfires can double in size every 30–60 minutes under dry, windy conditions. A fire that is 1 hectare at detection can be 10 hectares within two hours — and 100 hectares within four. The cost of suppression, the area burned, and the risk to lives all scale exponentially with delayed detection.

Traditional detection methods — satellite passes every 3–12 hours, lookout towers, routine patrols — create a gap between ignition and alert. By the time a fire appears on the next satellite pass, it may already be beyond initial attack. Citizen-powered detection fills this gap: someone on the ground can see and report a fire within minutes of ignition, long before any satellite overpass.

But speed alone isn't enough — the signal must be reliable. That's why Pyrae combines the speed of citizen reports with the reliability of AI screening and satellite corroboration. The result: fast and trustworthy detection.

Data sources in the pipeline

📸

Citizen photo reports

Ground-truth sightings from anyone with a phone. Each report includes a photo, GPS coordinates and timestamp — the raw evidence that feeds the pipeline.

🛰️

NASA FIRMS (VIIRS/MODIS)

Satellite thermal anomaly detections at 375 m resolution, refreshed every 3 hours. The primary corroboration source for confirming ground reports from space.

🌤️

Open-Meteo weather

Real-time wind, temperature and humidity data. Feeds the Bayesian spread model to predict where each fire will move next.

🗺️

OpenStreetMap terrain

Road networks, elevation and land-use data for road risk assessment and terrain-aware spread modelling.
