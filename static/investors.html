Pyrae — wildfire early detection

We're building an AI-powered wildfire early detection network that catches fires before they become large-scale disasters — combining citizen photo reports, live satellite data and predictive spread modelling into a single intelligence platform.

The problem

🔥

Wildfires are getting worse

Climate change is lengthening fire seasons, expanding the wildland-urban interface, and increasing the frequency and intensity of fire starts. The global cost of wildfires exceeds $50 billion annually — and the trend is accelerating.

⏱️

Existing detection is slow

Current detection methods — satellite passes every 3–12 hours, lookout towers, routine patrols — leave a dangerous gap between ignition and first alert.

💰

Suppression costs spiral

Every hour of delayed detection exponentially increases suppression costs and property loss. Early detection — catching fires when they're still small — is the single highest-leverage investment.

Our approach

📸

Citizen-powered detection

Anyone with a phone can report a suspected fire with a photo and GPS location. This creates a ground-truth detection network that complements satellite coverage.

🧠

AI-verified reports

Every photo is screened by a computer-vision model for visible fire and smoke. Only reports corroborated by satellite data reach the confirmed layer.

🛰️

Satellite corroboration

Live NASA FIRMS hotspot data validates ground reports from space. A photo sighting within 3 km of a satellite detection confirms the incident.

📈

Predictive spread modelling

A Bayesian fire grid models how each fire is likely to evolve — factoring in wind, terrain, vegetation and humidity.

Market opportunity

  • Global wildfire management market projected to reach $10+ billion by 2030.
  • Emergency services modernisation — agencies worldwide are investing in real-time intelligence platforms.
  • Insurance & utility sector demand — insurers need verified fire timelines; utilities need real-time risk data.
  • Wildland-urban interface expansion — more communities in fire-prone areas need early warning systems.
